# Audria — App Store Review Templates

Starting points for **real early-access users** to edit and post in their own words. Send one as a draft, ask them to change whatever isn't true for them, and let them post it from their own account.

Do not post these under accounts that aren't the reviewer's own. Both Apple and Google prohibit incentivized and company-authored reviews, and Audria is sold to a small, well-connected founder market — the same reasoning as §8 of the operating doc. A product about honoring what you committed to can't be marketed with manufactured proof.

Keep each under ~500 characters so the same text works on both stores. Titles run short — aim for under 50.

## Write to the tenure users actually have

We launched this week. Nobody has used Audria for more than a few days, so nothing below claims otherwise. That rules out the whole category of review that usually does the heavy lifting:

- No month counts, no "three months in", no "still using it".
- No retention claims — "the only tool that survived past week one" isn't available yet.
- No long-range recall examples. "What did we agree with that vendor in March" is impossible when the app has only ever seen this week's meetings. Ask it about Monday instead.
- No team-wide rollout results. One person, a handful of meetings, is the honest frame.

This is a constraint, not a weakness. A day-three review that says one specific true thing reads better than a vague rave, and a brand-new listing collecting reviews about months of use is exactly the pattern store fraud detection looks for. Rewrite these again in six weeks when the tenure claims become true.

Asked what we landed on in Monday's pricing call and got the decision plus the reasoning that got us there. Normally that's the part I lose — I remember the outcome and not the argument, so a month later we have the argument again. Three days in and I've started skipping my own notes. **★★★★★ Caught something I'd forgotten by lunch**

I'm in six to eight meetings a day and I leave most of them with a couple of verbal commitments I never write down. Audria handed two back to me the same afternoon, one I'd already lost. Been on it under a week so I can't speak to the long haul, but the recall problem is the real problem and this is the first thing aimed at it.

## Asking for the review

Send after they've had a specific win — they mentioned Audria surfaced something they'd have lost. At this stage that can happen on day two, so trigger on the win, not on a tenure threshold and not on a schedule.

> Hey [name] — you mentioned Audria caught the [thing] you'd committed to and forgotten. Would you be up for leaving that as an App Store review? Two or three sentences in your own words is plenty. I drafted something below as a starting point, but change anything that isn't how you'd actually put it — or ignore it entirely and write your own. Either way, no pressure and it doesn't affect anything on your account.
>
> [paste the closest template]

Never offer credits, extended trials, or anything else in exchange — that's an incentivized review under both stores' rules and it's the one thing that can get a listing pulled.

## Editing notes

- Specific beats abstract. "Handed two back to me the same afternoon" carries weight that "great product" doesn't.
- Name the limit. At this stage "too early to call it" is both true and more persuasive than certainty a three-day user couldn't have.
- Let the four-star ones through. A page of uniform five-star raves reads as bought; one honest gripe makes the rest credible.
- Vary the structure, not just the wording. Four reviews with the same problem-then-solution shape are visible as a set.
- If a user changes it heavily, that's the desired outcome, not a failure of the template.
