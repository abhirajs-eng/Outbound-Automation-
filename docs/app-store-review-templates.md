# Audria — App Store Review Templates

Starting points for **real early-access users** to edit and post in their own words. Send one as a draft, ask them to change whatever isn't true for them, and let them post it from their own account.

Do not post these under accounts that aren't the reviewer's own. Both Apple and Google prohibit incentivized and company-authored reviews, and Audria is sold to a small, well-connected founder market — the same reasoning as §8 of the operating doc. A product about honoring what you committed to can't be marketed with manufactured proof.

Keep each under ~500 characters so the same text works on both stores. Titles run short — aim for under 50.

---

## 1. Decisions — founder voice

**★★★★★ Stopped re-litigating decisions**

We'd decide something in a Tuesday meeting and by the following month nobody could reconstruct why. So we'd argue it again. Audria gives me the decision and the reasoning behind it in about ten seconds, and the argument doesn't happen a second time. That's the whole value for me — not the transcript, the *why*. Six people, three months in, and I've stopped keeping my own parallel notes.

---

## 2. Commitments — operator voice

**★★★★★ The things I said I'd do**

I'm in six to eight meetings a day and I used to leave every one of them with two or three verbal commitments I never wrote down. Half of them surfaced a week later when someone asked. Audria pulls them out and hands them back to me by end of day. The follow-through problem was never motivation, it was recall. First tool that actually fixed it.

---

## 3. Context — switched from a generic notetaker

**★★★★☆ Better than a transcript, still early**

I've used three AI notetakers. They all produce a summary I never read again. Audria is the first one that answers questions across meetings — "what did we agree with that vendor in March" gets a real answer, not a file to go dig through. Docked a star because search still misses when I phrase things loosely, and I'd like Slack context in there too. Using it daily regardless.

## 4. Short one — for people who won't write five sentences

**★★★★★ Meeting memory that actually works**

The context that usually evaporates between conversations just doesn't anymore. Ask it what we decided and why, get an answer. Been on it two months and it's the only meeting tool that survived past week one.

## Asking for the review

Send after they've had a specific win — they mentioned Audria surfaced something they'd have lost, or they've been active four-plus weeks. Not on a schedule.

> Hey [name] — you mentioned last week that Audria caught the [thing] you'd committed to and forgotten. Would you be up for leaving that as an App Store review? Two or three sentences in your own words is plenty. I drafted something below as a starting point, but change anything that isn't how you'd actually put it — or ignore it entirely and write your own. Either way, no pressure and it doesn't affect anything on your account.
>
> [paste the closest template]

Never offer credits, extended trials, or anything else in exchange — that's an incentivized review under both stores' rules and it's the one thing that can get a listing pulled.

## Editing notes

- Specific beats abstract. "Six people, three months in" carries weight that "great product" doesn't.
- Let the four-star ones through. A page of uniform five-star raves reads as bought; one honest gripe makes the rest credible.
- Vary the structure, not just the wording. Four reviews with the same problem-then-solution shape are visible as a set.
- If a user changes it heavily, that's the desired outcome, not a failure of the template.
